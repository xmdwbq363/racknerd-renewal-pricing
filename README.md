## Which tier is actually enough for your site?

Storage is the only variable here, so translate it into pages and visitors before choosing.

CNET's reference point for shared hosting is that 20 GB holds roughly 200 pages. That puts the 30 GB plan somewhere north of 300 pages — comfortably more than a personal blog, a portfolio, or a five-page business site will ever need, including images. Bandwidth is the more useful constraint: 2 TB per month is a long way past the 10,000 monthly visits that a $2.99 SiteGround StartUp plan budgets for. A text-and-image blog that gets 20,000 visits a month won't approach 2 TB.

- **One-page site, portfolio, CV, or a landing page:** 30 GB. Nothing above it buys you anything you'll use.
- **Personal or niche blog, up to a few hundred posts:** 30 GB still fits. Move up only if you're hosting large media files or video.
- **Small WooCommerce store:** 100 GB at $9.59/mo. Product photography and thumbnails accumulate faster than text, and you want headroom before a sales push rather than during one.
- **Multiple sites, membership content, or an image-heavy archive:** 200 GB at $15.59/mo. The unlimited-domains allowance means you can consolidate several small sites onto one account instead of paying for separate hosting.

"Unlimited domains and databases" comes with the usual reasonable-use and acceptable-use policies attached to every unlimited hosting offer. Read them if you plan to park dozens of domains or run resource-heavy scripts; the limits exist and they're enforced.

## What to confirm before you check out

1. **The billing term and the total charged today.** A monthly rate only becomes a budget once you multiply it by the term and add any setup or add-on fees.
2. **Whether the rate holds at renewal.** Ask support in writing if it isn't stated on the order page. This is the single question that separates a $67/year host from a $132/year host.
3. **What happens to your free SSL and backups if you downgrade or lapse.** Let's Encrypt certificates are free to reissue, but a lapsed account takes its JetBackup archives with it. Keep your own copy of anything you can't rebuild.
4. **The refund window and its conditions.** Get the current terms from RackNerd directly rather than from a review site, including this one — policies change and third-party summaries don't.
5. **Which datacenter you're being placed in.** US, Europe, and Asia locations are all offered; latency to your actual visitors matters more than the plan tier.
